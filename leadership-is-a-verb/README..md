Exercise #1: Leadership Autopsy

Take 15–20 minutes.

Choose three situations from your career where:

You were responsible for an outcome.
You lacked direct authority. (I also had impostor syndrome and massive trust issues formed by truly unbelievable situations)
The result was disappointing.

For each, write:

Situation

What happened?
- 2003: given implicit authority (not title)

Technical Solution

What did you think needed to happen?
- 2003: wedge myself into every situation as "senior" or "in charge"

Human System

Who needed to:

agree,
contribute,
approve,
care,
participate?
- 2003: Supervisor (just kind of ran with things, didn't get his feedback)
        Peers (tried to user orders instead of persuasion)
        Supervisor
        Everyone - orders don't achieve that; persuasion does (this is care and participate)

Missed Influence Opportunity

What could you have done differently?
Trust myself and others. Don't be afraid to "bother" others (especially the boss)

Don't beat yourself up.

We're building pattern recognition.

Exercise #3: The Question Ratio

For the rest of today:

In every meaningful conversation, observe:

How many questions do I ask?

versus

How many statements do I make?

Do not try to change anything yet.

Just measure.

You are gathering baseline data.
