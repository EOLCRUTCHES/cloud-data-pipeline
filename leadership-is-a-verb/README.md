LEADERSHIP IS A VERB is a 60-day self-exploration of potential and practical leadership, starting with a post-mortem of past missed leaderdship opportunities. The course originates from a concurrent 90-day professional development stack for data engineeers that is not relevant for all users. For that reason, the first day of this micro-learning journey is marked as Day 31.
### Objective

Recognize the difference between **creating value personally** and **creating value through other people**.

This is the foundational mindset shift for the entire Lead curriculum.

Day 30 (Introductory Read)
Leadership is a verb. It is easy to fall into any one of the following traps:
- Eagerness to climb
- People pleasing up, down, and across the organization
- Not bothering people, consequently hampering operational readiness and your image as a leader by losing sync with employers and employees
- Rejecting feedback via hostility, stonewalling, or other toxic habits 
- Checking boxes instead of exhibiting authentic yet persuasive engagement
- Taking tasking as a personal assignment rather than a delegable task
- Being bound by an org chart ("that person doesn't work for me, therefore they can't help me")
It is important to identify these and other toxic behaviors in one's own leadership style, not just at work but at home with family, friends, and in communities such as church or service organizations. Mental notes are fine, but it is recommended to write the post-mortem down as a starting point to refer back to.

## Day 31 - Relationship Maps Beat Org Charts

### Key Lesson
Authority and influence are different resources.
The org chart describes authority.
The relationship map describes influence.
High-leverage leaders learn both.

### Observation

People I expected to be influential:
- The highly-vocal past president
- The money man with a broad business network
- The member with the lengthy service club resume

People who actually appeared influential:
- Leaders in other clubs. 

## Lead Day 32 - Ask Before You Solve
### Key Lesson
Leadership often starts by asking better questions before offering answers.

### Practice Rule
Before giving a recommendation, ask at least two questions.

### Questions I Can Use
- What outcome are you hoping for?
- What have you already tried?
- What would make this easier?
- Who else needs to be involved?
- What does success look like?
- What constraint am I not seeing?

### Reflection
Where did I feel the urge to solve too quickly?
- Everywhere. Literally.
What did I learn by asking first?
- (Come back to this one)

## Lead Day 33 - Let Silence Work
### Key Lesson
Leadership is not only asking better questions. It is also leaving enough space for people to answer them.

### Practice Rule
Ask a question, stop talking, and count to five before adding anything.

### Questions I Can Use
- What do you think the real blocker is?
- Who else needs to be involved for this to move?
- What would make this easier to say yes to?
- What do you think the next step should be?

### Reflection
Where did I feel the urge to fill the silence?
What did the other person say when I waited?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I creating space for ownership, or am I taking ownership back by talking too much?

## Lead Day 34 - Executive Answer First
### Key Lesson
Gravitas improves when I lead with judgment instead of explanation.

### Practice Rule
Before giving technical detail, answer the decision first in three sentences or fewer.

### Phrases I Can Use
- The short version is...
- My recommendation is...
- The decision point is...
- The risk is...
- The main tradeoff is...

### Reflection
- Where did I give more detail than the decision required?
- Where did I successfully stop after the executive answer?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I answering the decision, or displaying everything I know?

## Lead Day 35 - Name the Decision
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I identify the actual decision before giving details.

### Practice Rule
Before explaining, say: "The decision is whether we should ___ or ___."

### Decision Types
- Approve or reject
- Start, stop, or continue
- Accept risk or reduce risk
- Spend money or save money
- Assign ownership or leave vague
- Move now or wait
- Escalate or handle locally

### Reflection
Where did I answer before naming the decision?
Where did naming the decision make the conversation clearer?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I helping the room decide, or am I just adding information?

## Lead Day 36 - Name the Tradeoff
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I make the hidden tradeoff visible.

### Practice Rule
Say: "The tradeoff is ___ versus ___."

### Useful Tradeoff Pairs
- Speed versus control
- Growth versus bandwidth
- Risk reduction versus schedule
- Technical quality versus business timing
- Short-term relief versus long-term stability
- Volunteer ambition versus volunteer capacity
- Compliance confidence versus operational momentum

### Reflection
Where did I see a tradeoff before speaking?
Where did naming the tradeoff make the decision clearer?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I helping people understand the cost of the choice, or am I just describing the situation?

## Lead Day 37 - Name the Next Move
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I end with a clear next action instead of more explanation.

### Practice Rule
Say: "The next move is for ___ to ___ by ___."

### Leadership Sequence
1. Name the decision.
2. Name the tradeoff.
3. Give the recommendation.
4. Name the next move.

### Reflection
Where did I leave a conversation with no clear owner or next action?
- Where didn't I?
Where did naming the next move create clarity?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I helping the group move, or am I only helping the group understand?

## Lead Day 38 - Make the Clean Ask
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I make clear, direct asks instead of implying what needs to happen.

### Practice Rule
Use this structure: "Can you ___ by ___ so we can ___?"

### Clean Ask Formula
1. Who
2. What
3. By when
4. Why it matters

### Reflection
Where did I imply an ask instead of making one?

Where did a clean ask make action easier?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I making it easy for someone to say yes and act?

## Lead Day 39 - Close the Loop
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I calmly follow up after making the ask.

### Practice Rule
Use the phrase: "Just closing the loop on ___."

### Useful Follow-Up Phrases
- Just closing the loop on ___.
- Are we still tracking toward ___ by ___?
- Do we have what we need to close this out?
- Is this still with you, or should we redirect it?
- What is the cleanest next step from here?

### Reflection
Where did I make an ask but fail to follow up?

Where did a calm follow-up help prevent drift?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I keeping the thread alive, or assuming agreement equals completion?

## Lead Day 40 - Escalate Without Drama
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I escalate the decision, risk, or blocker instead of blaming the person.

### Practice Rule
When something is stuck, lead with: "The open item is..." or "The blocker is..."

### Useful Phrases
- The open item is...
- The blocker is...
- The risk of waiting is...
- The decision we need is...
- The next move is...

### Reflection
Where did I avoid escalation because I did not want tension?
- Where didn't I?
Where could I have escalated the issue more calmly and earlier?
- Probably everywhere

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I raising the risk early, or waiting until it becomes an avoidable "aw shit"?
- Another trust issue - the fear of being seen as incompetent or untrustworthy - of being fired, demoted, or otherwise rejected.

## Lead Day 41 - Prewire the Room
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I check stakeholder reactions before the formal conversation.

### Practice Rule
Before a meaningful meeting or decision, ask one person: "What is your read on this before it comes up formally?"

### What I Am Listening For
- Support
- Resistance
- Confusion
- Hidden risk
- Politics
- Ownership gaps
- Better framing

### Useful Phrases
- I wanted to check your read before this comes up formally.
- What concern do you think people will have with this?
- What would make this easier to support?
- Who else needs context before this becomes a decision?

### Reflection
Where did I rely on the meeting instead of preparing the room?
- Literally everywhere
Where could an early conversation have prevented resistance or surprise?
- Probably everywhere. Early communications in the Rotary context are largely ignored.
### Carry-Forward Question

Am I walking into the room informed, or am I using the meeting to discover the room?

## Lead Day 42 - Find the Hidden Veto
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I identify who can quietly slow, stall, or kill an idea even without formal authority.

### Practice Rule
Before pushing an idea, ask: "Who needs to be comfortable with this before it can really move?"

### Hidden Veto Sources
- Respected veteran
- Operational realist
- Budget holder
- Quiet technical expert
- Burned-out volunteer
- Informal influencer
- Person who has seen this fail before

### Reflection
Where have I assumed formal approval was enough?

Who could quietly slow down something I am trying to move?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I only tracking who can approve this, or also who can stall it?

## Lead Day 43 - Translate Resistance Into Requirements
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I treat resistance as information instead of defiance.

### Practice Rule
When someone resists, ask: "What would need to be true for you to support this?"

### Resistance May Point To
- Trust in the plan
- Trust in the owner
- Timing
- Workload
- Risk
- Benefit
- Prior failed attempts

### Useful Phrases
- What would need to be true for this to work?
- What concern would we need to address first?
- Is the issue the idea, the timing, the owner, or the workload?
- What would make this easier to support?

### Reflection
Where did I defend instead of diagnose?

Where did resistance reveal a real requirement?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I trying to win the argument, or uncover the condition for support?

## Lead Day 44 - Build the Minimum Coalition
### Key Lesson
Leadership improves when I identify the smallest group of people needed to move an idea forward.

### Practice Rule
Before taking an idea wider, ask: "Who has to be aligned for this to actually happen?"

### Coalition Roles
- Sponsor
- Owner
- Expert
- Influencer
- Executor
- Possible blocker

### Reflection
Where have I tried to convince everyone before aligning the minimum coalition?

Which role is missing from something I am currently trying to move?

### Carry-Forward Question
Am I collecting general agreement, or building the small coalition required for movement?
